### 0.3 The non-negotiable "new route" ritual

Adding **any** route in this plan touches **five** files. Memorise this; it is the single most common source of drift in this repo:

1. `src/App.tsx` — `const X = lazy(() => import('./pages/...'))` **and** `<Route path="..." element={<X />} />`
2. `src/routeMeta.ts` — `{ path, title, description }`
3. `scripts/postbuild-ssg.mjs` — the **same** `{ path, title, description }` in its `ROUTES` array (plain JS, never imports TS)

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — THE FIVE STRATEGIC BETS

The source presentation (an internal Thales IAM town hall, senior-management strategy readout) communicates that the organisation's next phase of identity investment concentrates on **five bets**. Every one of them is an *industry-wide* direction that AboutIAM can teach vendor-neutrally, at depth, with Thales positioned as a flagship reference implementation.

| # | Theme | The one-line thesis | Portal priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **Agentic Identity & Governance** | AI agents are becoming first-class principals. An agent needs a job description, an owner, declared intent, and hard limits — i.e. it needs the full identity lifecycle humans already have. | **P1 — highest.** The flagship bet. |
| **T2** | **AI Security Fabric (AI Control Plane)** | Issuing an agent an identity is not enough. You need a runtime control plane that observes what the agent *actually does*, detects task drift and prompt manipulation, blocks sensitive egress, and can intervene. | **P1 — highest.** Inseparable twin of T1. |
| **T3** | **Phishing-Resistant Auth & FIDO Device Fleets** | FIDO is the settled answer to phishing. The unsolved problem is **operating it at fleet scale**: form-factor choice, personalisation, distribution, lifecycle, helpdesk economics, and crypto-agility of the hardware itself. | **P2 — high.** Large existing base to deepen. |
| **T4** | **Digital Wallets & Verifiable Credentials** | Wallet-based identity is proven at national scale and is becoming a *legal obligation* for relying parties in the EU. The full lifecycle matters: verification, issuance, and the under-served **organisational (business) wallet**. | **P2 — high.** Regulatory clock is the hook. |
| **T5** | **Crypto Agility, PQC & Root of Trust** | Every theme above rests on cryptography with a finite shelf life. Post-quantum migration and hardware-anchored trust (HSM-backed roots) are the cross-cutting substrate. | **P3 — cross-cutting.** Binds T1–T4. |

### 1.1 The single most reusable idea in the source material

The presentation frames agentic identity across **four ecosystem quadrants**, and this is the most pedagogically valuable construct in the entire deck. It becomes a first-class, reusable model on the portal (§5.3, §7.2.2):

| Quadrant | Who acts | Illustrative ask | Core identity problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Enterprise** | Agents inside the organisation's own environment | *"Check incoming claims for missing information."* | Workload-style identity, ownership, blast radius |
| **2. Modern Workforce** | Employees and their agents working together | *"My agent team drafts a recommendation; I decide."* | Human-in-the-loop, delegated authority, accountability |
| **3. Business Partners** | Partner / seller agents reaching shared services | *"Submit a claim on behalf of my brokerage's client."* | Cross-org federation, on-behalf-of chains, trust registries |
| **4. Consumers** | End users bringing *their own* agents to your service | *"File a claim for my stolen bicycle."* | Consent, scoped authority, revocation, liability |

This matrix is the organising spine of the Agentic Identity Center and of the new Delegation Chain playground. Teaching it well is the highest-leverage single deliverable in this plan.

### 1.2 The second most reusable idea: the two-column governance split

The deck splits agent governance into two complementary control surfaces. This split is the conceptual backbone of §5.3 and §5.4 and should be taught as a *pair*, never separately:

| **Agent Identity** (design-time / issuance) | **AI Security Fabric** (run-time / enforcement) |
|---|---|
| Who is this agent? | What is the agent actually doing? |
| Who is it working for (owner / principal)? | Is it staying within the task it was given? |
| What is it allowed to do? | Is someone trying to steer it off course? |
| What data and services can it access? | Is sensitive information leaving the business? |
| Under what circumstances? | When should we step in? |
| **Capabilities:** agent registry, agent identity & ownership, declared agent intent, MCP tool + data protection | **Capabilities:** discovery, AI firewall, semantic guardrails, policy, observability, data protection, dashboard |

Pedagogically: the left column is **AuthN/AuthZ for a new principal type**; the right column is **runtime authorization + DLP + behavioural detection**. Learners who already understand IAM will grasp agentic identity fastest through exactly this mapping (§5.3.4 makes it explicit).

### 1.3 What "done" looks like

On completion the portal gains:

- **1 new top-level pillar** (`/next-gen`) with **6 hub pages**

### 2.4 Citation discipline — the `verifiedVia` rule

**Every factual claim carrying a number, a date, a ranking or an attribution must ship with its source.** This is already the house style — imitate `complianceDeadlines.ts` and `reportsRegistry.ts` exactly.

Mandatory fields on any new registry entry that asserts a fact:

```typescript
/** Public URL that substantiates this entry. */
officialLink: string        // or sourceLink / link
/** ISO date you personally fetched and read that URL. */
verifiedDate: string        // 'YYYY-MM-DD'
/** 'confirmed' = stated directly in an enacted regulation/published spec/primary source.
 *  'estimated' | 'partial' = depends on implementing acts, phased rollout, draft status,
 *  or is corroborated by only a single interested party. */
confidence: 'confirmed' | 'estimated'
/** Optional but preferred: what exactly you cross-checked, in one sentence. */
verifiedVia?: string
```

**Hard rule:** if you cannot fetch a public URL that substantiates a number, **do not write the number**. Write the mechanism instead. A page that explains *how* wallet acceptance obligations work is more valuable — and infinitely more defensible — than one asserting an unsourced market size.

Every new registry must carry a header comment in house style stating what it is, how it is sourced, and its review cadence. Pattern to copy, from `walletAdoptionTracker.ts`:

```typescript
/**
 * <What this registry is and what question it answers.>
 * Hand-curated in the same spirit as complianceDeadlines.ts. Every entry records
 * the public source it was checked against as of `verifiedDate` — re-verify before
 * relying on any of it. Educational starting point, not legal or compliance advice.
 * Refresh cadence: quarterly. Last reviewed: <YYYY-MM-DD>.
 */
```

### 2.6 Vendor-neutrality contract (approved positioning: neutral-first, Thales as flagship)

AboutIAM's credibility — and its SEO performance — depend on being genuinely educational rather than promotional. The approved model:

1. **Teach the standard first.** Every new page leads with the problem, the standards, the threat model and a hands-on lab. A learner with no interest in any vendor must find the page complete and useful.
2. **Then surface vendors comparatively.** Use the existing `enterpriseProducts` string convention already present across `PlaygroundCatalog.tsx` entries, naming Thales *alongside* Microsoft Entra, Okta, Ping, Keycloak, CyberArk, SailPoint and others, exactly as existing entries do.
3. **Thales gets flagship depth where it is already established.** `vendorCatalog.ts` already carries `isFeatured: true` and a `THALES_PRODUCTS` array (OneWelcome, SafeNet Trusted Access, IdCloud). Extend *that* structure with public capability detail per theme — this is the sanctioned promotional surface.
4. **No new `/thales` marketing route.** (Explicitly considered and declined in planning.) A vendor-specific hub page on an open-source neutral platform would read as advertising, would attract community pushback, and would dilute the topical authority that makes the neutral pages rank. The flagship treatment inside `/vendor` achieves the commercial goal without that cost.
5. **Never disparage a competitor.** `limitations` fields must be factual and sourced, applied evenly across all vendors including Thales.
6. **Never claim a capability that is not publicly documented.** If Thales public material does not describe a capability, it does not go in the registry — regardless of what the internal deck says is coming.

**Net commercial effect:** the portal becomes the deepest free educational resource on exactly the five themes Thales is investing in. Practitioners researching "how do I govern AI agent identity" arrive at a page that teaches them properly and shows Thales as a credible flagship implementer. That is durable, high-intent demand generation; a brochure page is not.

#### 5.3.1 Tab `why` — Why Agents Break Classic IAM

Content: five concrete ways agents violate assumptions baked into existing IAM:

| Classic assumption | How an agent breaks it | Consequence |
|---|---|---|

### 7.2 New playgrounds (10)

#### 7.2.1 Agent Registry & Lifecycle Studio — `/playground/agent-registry` ⭐
**Teaches:** an agent needs a job description, an owner, declared intent and a lifecycle.
**Flow:** (1) Pick a sample agent from `agentRegistryModel.ts`. (2) See its identity record rendered as a form, with `governanceGaps` hidden. (3) **Audit it** — flag missing owner, absent expiry, over-broad authority, undeclared intent, no revocation trigger. Score against the seeded gaps. (4) Fix each gap; watch a live "governance posture" meter respond. (5) Run the lifecycle: register → approve → operate → review → revoke → decommission, with the trace terminal logging each state transition and what a real system would emit. (6) Export the completed record as JSON.
**Twist:** one sample agent is *over-governed* — so restricted it cannot do its job. Teaches that governance has a usability cost.
**Data:** `agentRegistryModel.ts`. **moduleId:** `agent_registry_studio`.

#### 7.2.2 Delegation Chain Auditor — `/playground/delegation-chain` ⭐
**Teaches:** multi-hop on-behalf-of authority, the confused-deputy problem, attribution.
**Flow:** (1) Pick a quadrant from `agenticEcosystemQuadrants.ts` — the quadrant changes the trust boundaries, which is the point. (2) Build a chain: user → orchestrator agent → sub-agent → tool → downstream API. (3) At each hop choose a delegation mechanism (token exchange RFC 8693, RAR-constrained token, CIBA human approval, raw scope forwarding). (4) The visualiser shows authority *narrowing or widening* at each hop, with widening flagged red. (5) Inject failures: a hop that drops the `act` claim (attribution lost), a hop that forwards the original broad token (confused deputy), a cross-org hop with no trust-registry check. (6) Produce an audit trail and ask the learner: *who is accountable for the final action?*
**Data:** `agenticEcosystemQuadrants.ts`, `agentThreatCatalog.ts`. **moduleId:** `delegation_chain_auditor`.

#### 7.2.3 AI Guardrail Policy Studio — `/playground/ai-guardrails` ⭐
**Teaches:** semantic guardrails vs structured policy; probabilistic enforcement.
**Flow:** (1) Author guardrails in a simple declarative form: intent bounds ("only answer billing questions"), tool allow/deny, data-class egress rules, rate/spend caps. (2) Run a corpus of ~20 simulated agent requests — some legitimate, some drifting, some hostile. (3) See per-request decisions with the triggering rule. (4) **Confusion matrix**: true/false positives and negatives. (5) Tighten rules and watch false positives rise as false negatives fall — the central honest lesson: a semantic guardrail is a tuned classifier, not a boolean policy. (6) Compare against a classic ABAC policy on the same corpus to show what each catches and misses.
**Data:** new `aiGuardrailScenarios.ts` (bundle with this lab). **moduleId:** `ai_guardrail_studio`.

#### 7.2.4 Agent Behavior Observability Lab — `/playground/agent-observability`
**Teaches:** task drift, intent-vs-action comparison, when to intervene.
**Flow:** (1) An agent is assigned a declared task. (2) Watch a timeline of its actual actions stream in. (3) A drift score computes from declared-intent vs observed-action divergence. (4) Set intervention thresholds on the ladder (log → warn → require approval → throttle → block → revoke). (5) Replay: see which rung fires when, and the cost of each — intervene too early and you break a legitimate task; too late and the damage is done. (6) Include a session-revocation event (CAEP/SSF-style) and show the propagation lag.
**Data:** new `agentDriftScenarios.ts`. **moduleId:** `agent_observability_lab`.

#### 7.2.5 Prompt Injection → Privilege Escalation Lab — `/playground/prompt-injection-escalation`
**Teaches:** the full identity-relevant chain from a hostile instruction to real privilege abuse. Differentiated from the existing `AIThreatLab` by being strictly about the **identity escalation path**.
**Flow:** Staged chain — injected instruction in retrieved content → agent's intent subverted → agent invokes a tool it is permitted to use → that tool's token is over-broad → lateral access achieved → exfiltration. At each stage offer a control (input provenance, intent re-validation, tool scope narrowing, token exchange downscoping, egress DLP) and re-run to show which stages each control actually closes. Key lesson: **narrow token scope stops the chain even when the injection succeeds** — identity controls are the durable mitigation when the model layer fails.
**Data:** `agentThreatCatalog.ts`. **moduleId:** `prompt_injection_escalation`.

#### 7.2.6 FIDO Fleet Operations Simulator — `/playground/fido-fleet-ops` ⭐
**Teaches:** running an authenticator fleet at scale — the genuinely unserved T3 gap.
**Flow:** Multi-round simulation over a modelled population (e.g. 5,000 users across office/remote/shared-device/privileged/contractor segments). Per round the learner allocates form factors and operational budget; events fire (bulk onboarding wave, lost-device spike, an authenticator model losing certification, a shared-device shift-work problem, a contractor surge, a device end-of-life refresh). Outcomes track enrolment coverage, phishing-resistant coverage of privileged accounts, helpdesk ticket volume, unit + fulfilment cost, and user satisfaction. Trace terminal logs each decision's downstream effect.
**Key lesson:** no single form factor fits a whole organisation; segmentation is the skill.
**Data:** `fidoFormFactors.ts`, `fidoFleetLifecycle.ts`. **moduleId:** `fido_fleet_ops`.

#### 7.2.7 AAGUID & Attestation Policy Lab — `/playground/attestation-policy`
**Teaches:** enterprise attestation, AAGUID allow-listing, certification-level enforcement.
**Flow:** (1) Author an authenticator policy: allowed AAGUIDs, required certification level, attestation conveyance, resident-key and user-verification requirements. (2) Present a stream of registration attempts from varied simulated authenticators. (3) See accept/reject with the exact policy clause responsible. (4) Explore failure modes: policy so strict that legitimate users cannot enrol; allow-list going stale as models are superseded; privacy trade-off of attestation. (5) Decode a sample attestation object, cross-linking `/tools/webauthn-decoder`.
**Data:** `fidoFormFactors.ts` + new `attestationPolicyScenarios.ts`. **moduleId:** `attestation_policy_lab`.

#### 7.2.8 Business Wallet Studio — `/playground/business-wallet` ⭐
**Teaches:** organisations as credential holders — the biggest T4 gap.
**Flow:** (1) Choose a use case from `businessWalletUseCases.ts`. (2) Set up the org wallet: custody model (who holds the keys), delegation (which employees may present which credentials), multi-signer authority thresholds for high-value presentations, audit requirements. (3) Receive credentials into the org wallet (company registration, licence). (4) Delegate presentation authority to an employee and watch the authority chain form. (5) Present to a verifier; the verifier checks issuer trust, revocation status, and holder binding. (6) Inject problems: an employee leaves while holding delegated authority (revocation propagation); a credential is revoked mid-transaction; a delegation exceeds its threshold.
**Data:** `businessWalletUseCases.ts`. **moduleId:** `business_wallet_studio`.

#### 7.2.9 Credential Issuance Studio — `/playground/credential-issuance`
**Teaches:** the issuer role — absent from the portal today.
**Flow:** (1) Define a credential type and schema. (2) Configure issuance: OpenID4VCI flow shape, holder binding, signing key (cross-link root-of-trust), validity window. (3) Set up revocation (status list) and explain the privacy trade-off between revocation checkability and verifier-side correlation. (4) Issue to a simulated holder wallet. (5) Verify from the verifier side. (6) Revoke and observe propagation. (7) Trade-off exercise: long validity + revocation list vs short validity + reissuance.
**Data:** existing `openId4VcScenarios.ts` extended. **moduleId:** `credential_issuance_studio`.

#### 7.2.10 Crypto Migration Planner — `/playground/crypto-migration`
**Teaches:** dependency-ordered crypto agility.
**Flow:** (1) Inventory: select which identity components exist in your estate. (2) The planner computes HNDL exposure per component. (3) Learner sequences migration workstreams onto a timeline. (4) **Dependency violations are flagged** — e.g. migrating credential signatures before the issuing CA, or authenticator attestation before hardware supports the algorithm. (5) Budget and hardware-refresh-cycle constraints apply. (6) Output: a dependency-valid roadmap, exportable. Cross-links `/tools/pqc-readiness-auditor`.
**Data:** `cryptoAgilityRoadmap.ts`, `pqcAlgorithmRisk.ts`. **moduleId:** `crypto_migration_planner`.

## 10. CONTENT QUALITY BAR

The portal's existing content is unusually good. Match it or do not ship.

1. **Dual-audience always.** Every concept gets a genuine `analogy` (a real-world comparison a non-specialist grasps immediately) *and* an `expert` paragraph with the precise technical claim plus spec reference. Read 20 existing `encyclopediaData.ts` entries before writing your first one.
2. **Teach the problem before the solution.** `standardsData.ts` has `problem` and `whyExists` fields for a reason. A learner who does not understand the problem cannot evaluate the solution.
3. **Be honest about limitations.** Semantic guardrails are probabilistic. Passkey sync has enterprise trade-offs. PQC signatures are larger. Wallet revocation has privacy costs. The existing content's credibility comes from this candour — preserve it.
4. **Every threat gets a mitigation.** Never leave a learner with a scare and no remedy.
5. **Every lab teaches a transferable lesson**, not just mechanics. State it explicitly in the lab's completion state.
6. **Date everything volatile.** Agentic-identity standards are moving monthly. Any draft reference carries its status and `verifiedDate`.
7. **No marketing voice.** No "revolutionary", "game-changing", "industry-leading". Describe mechanisms.
8. **Accessibility is not optional.** Keyboard-navigable, ARIA-labelled, `jest-axe`-clean, works at 320px, sufficient contrast in both themes.
